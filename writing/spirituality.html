On religion and spirituality

There’s some really valuable advice across many religions, and lots of beautiful analogies. But a big part of religion is faith, and I’d say to abandon blind faith — depending on the values a religion instills, faith can harm other people quite a bit. We also need to understand the contexts in which these books were written; the way we live today is probably quite different from life thousands of years ago.

To me, spirituality is about choosing good objective functions — and learning how to change them when needed, and stick to them when others don’t believe in you. Some think spirituality is about going to a forest in India and meditating for years. That will definitely reduce your own suffering and make you aware of very minute things about your body and mind. But unless you can telepathically reduce other people’s suffering, you’ve only reduced your own; in the years spent meditating, you could also have reduced suffering for others more directly.

We evolved from creatures driven by instinct and hormones. Our gift is that we can make intentional decisions and be aware of our own emotions. The most evolved creatures would be the ones that are very aware — of physics, chemistry, people, the mechanics of things, the economy — and who use that knowledge to make the world better. To me, a perfect society is a group of humans that is incredibly intentional in what they do.
