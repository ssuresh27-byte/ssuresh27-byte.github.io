Why I do what I do

I decided to study computer science for quite shallow reasons; I heard that people who did a CS major generally made more money. From there I was trying to find some CS subject that resonated with my background in science, and I got interested in quantum computing, since it related to my prior background in math. I in general never loved programming in college even though I studied CS. To me it felt tedious, and I would much rather tell someone what to program than spend hours on the syntax myself. I guess that’s why programming became so much more fun when coding models like Opus came out. A lot of what I like to program are tools that make learning more efficient, more interesting, and more accessible.

One reason I find quantum to be an interesting area of research is that it is a hot field — people care about it because a quantum era changes a lot of things. I also like it because it brings together a lot of subjects I’m interested in, and because the quantum-computing stack doesn’t have such rigid boundaries. People working on the higher levels of the stack still need to be aware of lower-level details like error correction, or even hardware implementation. This forces researchers to be quite knowledgeable across many areas.

Growing up, some of us have the impression that the things we are naturally interested in are the things we should pursue. College changed that for me. I was naturally interested in math and physics, but I felt reluctant to study them purely — mainly because going into academia in those fields felt challenging, and I worried I wouldn’t be making a substantial impact. So I chose computer science. I honestly did not like it for the first few years; it felt like many of the choices programming-language creators made were for users and not for mathematical or structural elegance, and that made it hard to learn.

I also got super invested in Carnatic music in college. I think being good at things makes you want to be the best, and a lot of my time went into that. Where does it fit into my life now? Honestly, it’s not something I see as a career anymore. It’s just a genre of music I like a lot and enjoy practicing. I’ve put in enough effort that, thankfully, I can still make a bit of money from it through concerts and teaching — but the non-monetary value for me greatly exceeds the monetary value.
