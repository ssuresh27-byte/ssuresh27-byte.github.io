The onion

People have drastically different lenses through which they view others. Some are surprisingly shallow and see people as their physical appearance; others look at achievements or status; others look at how genuine someone is. Regardless, I think it’s important to understand people and the lenses they view others through. You may have a beautiful inner core, but if the outer layer of your onion isn’t visually pleasing, you may never get the chance to share that core.

I think there’s no harm in altering how you present yourself based on your audience — you’d talk to a well-known faculty member differently than to a teenager. Part of the reason, though, is that many people are shallow: if everyone could see past the first few layers of the onion, we wouldn’t have to buy new clothes to keep up with trends. I used to not worry about my outer layers, assuming people would eventually recognize the inner ones given enough time. But that’s only true if they want to be around you long enough for it to happen — and for that, you sometimes need to appeal to the short-sighted.
